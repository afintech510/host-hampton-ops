# Independent Contractor (Subcontractor) Agreement — Party Crew

*Version v1. Names and addresses are filled in from each crew member's own profile when the agreement is generated.*

This agreement is between Host Hampton, 295 Montauk Hwy, Speonk, NY 11972, and the crew member named on the signed copy.

## 1. The services

Host Hampton runs kids' parties and events in its studio and at families' homes. The Contractor may choose to work those parties as part of the party crew: welcoming families, setting up, running the activities, and cleaning up. A party is posted in the Host Hampton crew app with its date, time, location and role (Party Lead or Party Crew) before the Contractor decides whether to take it.

## 2. No minimum, no guarantee

Host Hampton offers parties; the Contractor may accept any of them or decline any of them, for any reason, with no penalty and no minimum. Host Hampton does not promise any amount of work. Once the Contractor has chosen a party, Host Hampton counts on them to be there, and the cancellation terms in section 5 explain how a late cancellation is handled.

## 3. Rate and method of pay

For each party the Contractor works, Host Hampton pays a flat amount for that party (not an hourly wage). The amount for a party is shown to the Contractor in the crew app before they accept it, and follows the published schedule in the app, which may include extra pay for a particular party. Tips that clients give the crew are the Contractor's, in addition to the flat amount.

Host Hampton pays by Venmo, Zelle, cash or check, as recorded in the crew app. Host Hampton may change the published schedule for future parties, and will never reduce the amount shown for a party the Contractor has already accepted.

## 4. When payment is due, and how the work is submitted

Payment for a party is due on the day of the party, once attendance for it has been recorded in the crew app. The Contractor submits what they worked through the crew app: taking a spot, confirming it, and being marked as having attended. If the Contractor believes a party was recorded wrongly or payment is missing, they should tell Host Hampton within 30 days so it can be corrected.

## 5. Cancelling a party you accepted

If the Contractor can no longer work a party they accepted, they should release it in the crew app as early as they can. A release made far ahead is handled with no consequence beyond the party returning to the board. A late cancellation that another crew member covers is handled without penalty. A late cancellation that no one covers, or not showing up, may affect the Contractor's place on the published rate schedule for future parties, as described in the app. Host Hampton may stop offering parties to someone who repeatedly fails to show up.

## 6. How the work is done

The Contractor is an independent contractor and decides how to do the work, subject to the party plan and the safety and conduct rules in section 8, which exist to protect children and the client's event. The Contractor may work for other people and businesses, including similar ones, at any time. The Contractor supplies their own transportation and their own phone.

## 7. Taxes, no benefits

The Contractor is responsible for their own taxes on what they earn. Host Hampton does not withhold taxes and will report payments to the Contractor on a Form 1099 where the law requires, using a Form W-9 that the Contractor provides separately; this agreement does not collect any tax identification number. The Contractor is not an employee and is not entitled to employee benefits, overtime, paid leave or workers' compensation from Host Hampton by reason of this agreement.

## 8. Child safety and conduct

The Contractor will treat every child and family with respect; will never be alone with a child out of sight of another adult or the parent; will not use, or arrive under the influence of, alcohol or drugs; will not use physical discipline; and will report any injury, accident, safety concern or complaint to the Party Lead or to Host Hampton straight away. The Contractor confirms they are at least 18 years old and that nothing in their background would make it unsafe for them to work with children. Host Hampton may remove a Contractor from a party, and stop offering parties to them, at any time for safety or conduct reasons.

## 9. Confidentiality and photos of children

The Contractor will keep private what they learn about clients, families and children (names, addresses, phone numbers, allergies, medical or family information) and will use it only to do the party. The Contractor will not photograph or film children at a party for their own use, and will not post any image of a child, or any detail that identifies one, to social media or anywhere else. Photos for Host Hampton's own marketing are taken only by Host Hampton under its own consent process.

## 10. Supplies and equipment

Host Hampton supplies the party materials. The Contractor will look after them, use them only for Host Hampton parties, and return anything left over after a party.

## 11. Clients are not to be solicited

While the Contractor is working with Host Hampton, and for 12 months after their last party, they will not use Host Hampton's client contact details to offer a competing party service to a family they met through Host Hampton, or ask such a family to book them directly instead. This does not stop the Contractor from working for anyone else, or from serving people who find them without that contact.

## 12. Ending this agreement

Either side may end this agreement at any time, for any reason, by telling the other (a text or email is enough). Parties the Contractor has already accepted remain subject to section 5 unless Host Hampton releases them. Sections 7, 9 and 11 continue after the agreement ends.

## 13. General

This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of New York. It is the whole agreement between the parties about this subject and replaces anything said earlier; a change must be in writing, and Host Hampton may offer a new version, which applies once the Contractor signs it. If any part is found unenforceable, the rest stays in force. The parties agree that signing electronically has the same effect as signing on paper, and that signing is the Contractor's own act.
